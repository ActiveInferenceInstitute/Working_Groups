## ARTICLE I: NAME AND AFFILIATION

**Section 1.1. Name.** These working groups operate under the name "Active Inference Institute Working Groups" (collectively, the "AII Working Groups" or "Working Groups").[^2]

**Section 1.2. Convener.** The Active Inference Institute, a 501(c)(3) nonprofit organization, is the convening and publishing organization for the Working Groups. Working Groups are member-constituted bodies under this Charter. While AII serves as convener, Working Groups operate in accordance with this Charter, any supplementary Operating Procedures adopted pursuant to Article X, and AII institutional policies applicable to AII-published outputs. Withdrawal of AII as convener does not dissolve a Working Group. The AII Board may not dissolve a Working Group.[^3]

## ARTICLE II: PURPOSE

**Section 2.1. Purpose.** The purpose of the Working Groups is to operate a Field Working Group and domain working groups. The Active Inference Field Working Group maintains a terminology correspondence note, a public comparison protocol for independently authored implementations, and a shared domain-assessment framework agreed with constituted domain groups. Domain working groups produce literacy and adaptation materials, an initial domain assessment against that framework, and a published go/no-go on the group’s continuation. The number of domain groups constituted at founding is the number of non-deferred candidate domains that meet the formation floor and the domain participation conditions in Article III.4. Confirmation of the observations in Section 2 of the accompanying Position Paper is outside the authorized purpose of the Working Groups and is not a continuation warrant.[^4]

**Section 2.2. Year-one outputs.** Authorized year-one outputs differ for the Field Working Group and for domain groups. The Field Working Group is authorized to produce: (a) a terminology correspondence note; (b) a public comparison protocol, including at least one worked example; (c) a shared domain-assessment framework, reviewed and agreed with constituted domain groups; and (d) an external-review record for (a) and (b). The Field Working Group may add independently authored implementations to the comparison set in its Year One Work Plan without a Charter amendment. Each domain working group is authorized to produce: (a) an audience and use-case note; (b) one literacy or adaptation module; (c) participation in the Field Working Group's framework series and an initial domain assessment against the agreed framework; and (d) a published go/no-go on the group’s continuation. A Working Group may add further work of the same type in its Year One Work Plan if the addition does not displace the named products. Changing the named year-one object, adding a new class of work, adding a second domain, or undertaking a certificate, tool-ownership, or standards product requires rechartering. A domain roadmap is authorized as subsequent-year work for a domain working group that has completed its initial assessment; it requires a new annual Work Plan decision. Subsequent years are a new decision.[^5]

**Section 2.3. Publication and access.** The Active Inference Institute is the exclusive official publisher of Working Group outputs produced under its institutional imprint. Official outputs are provided free of charge to all Working Group participants. Individual authors may publish their own independent papers, including papers that cite or build upon published Working Group outputs, in any venue. Unless the AII Board of Directors, upon recommendation of the relevant Working Group and with documented justification, authorizes a different access term for a specific output, official outputs are released to the public under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0). The terminology correspondence matrix, the comparison protocol, and the assessment framework shall remain publicly available at no charge. The Board may authorize a nominal charge to non-participants for other official outputs.[^6]

**Section 2.4. Categories of authority.** Authority within the Working Group system operates in three categories. Administrative authority—including budget allocation, institutional policies, IP policy, decisions to withdraw AII as convener, and AII institutional representations—rests with the AII Board of Directors. Substantive responsibility—including responsibility for technical analysis, domain assessments, interpretation of evidence, and conclusions contained in Working Group deliverables—rests with the Working Group members operating through the consensus process established in this Charter. Consensus under this Charter determines whether a text may be published as a Working Group position. It does not establish scientific consensus in the Active Inference field or independent scientific validation. Shared or appealable authority—including publication decisions, Charter amendments, and the interpretation of Charter provisions in specific cases—requires both Working Group action and AII Board concurrence as specified in the relevant articles of this Charter. Working Group members who believe the AII Board has exercised administrative authority in a manner that interferes with Working Group substantive responsibility may appeal in writing to the AII Board; the Board shall respond in writing within thirty days. Where the dispute involves a claim that AII administrative action has materially compromised Working Group substantive independence, the appealing Working Group members may request independent review by a three-person panel composed of: one member selected by the Working Group, one member selected by the AII Board, and one member agreed upon by both parties within fifteen days. The panel's findings are advisory to the AII Board but shall be published alongside the Board's response in AII's public records.[^7]

**Section 2.5. Independent continuation tests.** Evidence that supports the Field Working Group—including the existence of independently authored implementations and the absence of a public comparison protocol—shall not be treated as a warrant for constituting or continuing a domain working group. Domain participation in the assessment-framework series is collaboration on method and is likewise not a continuation warrant for the Field group or for any domain group. Each domain group must meet the formation floor and the domain participation conditions in Article III.4 on its own.[^8]

## ARTICLE III: MEMBERSHIP

**Section 3.1. Open membership.** Working Group membership is open to any individual with relevant expertise or substantive interest who agrees to abide by this Charter, the Working Group Operating Procedures, and AII's Conflict of Interest Policy. Membership is open to critics of active inference and to domain practitioners who are not proponents of the framework.[^9]

**Section 3.2. Institute membership not required.** Membership in a Working Group does not require membership in the Active Inference Institute, though AII community membership is encouraged. Membership confers no financial compensation from AII unless separately authorized by the AII Board.[^10]

**Section 3.3. Minimum active membership.** Each Working Group shall maintain a minimum of five active members to remain in operation. A member is "active" if they have attended at least one meeting and contributed to at least one deliverable activity in the preceding six months.[^11]

**Section 3.4. Formation Floor and Product-Fit Participation.** A Working Group shall not be constituted at the founding Symposium, or thereafter, unless at least five individuals have committed in writing to the group's year-one product and the applicable participation conditions in this section are met. Five is the number proposed to the Symposium. The Symposium may amend this number in the provisional Charter. After constitution, this number may be changed only under Article X.[^12]

**(a) Field Working Group.** The five written commitments must include at least one individual who maintains or actively uses an implementation included in the initial comparison set. Representation from every named implementation is not required at founding.[^13]

**(b) Domain working groups.** The five written commitments must include at least one active-inference expert and at least one domain practitioner who is actively working in the domain. An individual who is both an active-inference expert and actively working in the domain may satisfy both role requirements and counts as one of the five. An active-inference expert who is not actively working in the domain does not count as the domain practitioner. If a candidate domain has five written commitments but no active-inference expert, an SAB member or other active-inference expert may volunteer to join as a regular member under Section 3.1 to supply that role. SAB members join that role by volunteering. They are not assigned.[^14]

**(c) Suggestion list.** Interest below the floor, or a candidate that meets the numeric floor but not the product-fit conditions, leaves the domain on the suggestion list. Every non-deferred candidate domain that independently meets the floor and the domain conditions shall be constituted. Domains that do not meet those conditions at founding remain on the suggestion list and may form later when they meet them.[^15]

**Section 3.5. Multiple-group participation.** Members may participate in more than one Working Group. An individual serving in a leadership role (Chair or Vice Chair) in one Working Group may not simultaneously hold a leadership role in a second Working Group without approval from the AII Liaison.[^16]

**Section 3.6. SAB participation.** SAB members of the Active Inference Institute may participate in Working Groups in an advisory capacity. SAB participation does not carry voting authority on governance or budget matters, consistent with SAB members' advisory role under AII's governing documents. SAB members who separately join a Working Group as regular members under Section 3.1 participate in that capacity and may vote.[^17]

**Section 3.7. Termination.** Membership may be terminated by a two-thirds vote of the Working Group for conduct inconsistent with this Charter, the Operating Procedures, or AII's policies. The affected member shall be notified in writing and given an opportunity to respond before any vote is taken.[^18]

## ARTICLE IV: OFFICERS

**Section 4.1. Officers.** Each Working Group shall elect a Chair and a Vice Chair from among its active members. Elections are held at or before the first regular meeting of each calendar year. Officers serve one-year terms and may be re-elected for up to two consecutive terms in the same role, after which they must stand down for at least one term before becoming eligible again.[^19]

**Section 4.1(a). Vacancy and inactivity.** If a constituted Working Group has not elected a Chair and a Vice Chair at or before its first regular meeting, it remains constituted but is inactive until those officers are elected. If the vacancies persist for ninety days after constitution, the group is paused. Willing officers are a condition of activity.[^20]

**Section 4.2. Chair.** The Chair is responsible for convening meetings, setting agendas in consultation with the Working Group, facilitating deliberations, ensuring that action items and decisions are recorded, and reporting to AII as specified in Article VII.[^21]

**Section 4.3. Vice Chair.** The Vice Chair assists the Chair and assumes the Chair's responsibilities in the Chair's absence. The Vice Chair leads at least one substantive workstream each year.[^22]

**Section 4.4. AII Liaison.** The AII Board of Directors shall designate an AII Liaison to the Working Groups. The AII Liaison is not a voting member of any Working Group but attends meetings, provides institutional guidance, and serves as the primary point of coordination between the Working Groups and the AII Board.[^23]

**Section 4.5. Founding Period Continuity.** From the Working Groups' initial constitution through December 31, 2029 (the Founding Period), the AII Board of Directors may authorize an extension of a Chair's or Vice Chair's consecutive-term limits under Section 4.1, upon recommendation of the relevant Working Group and concurrence of the AII Liaison. Any extension under this provision shall be documented with the Board's stated justification and shall not extend service in the same role beyond the end of the Founding Period without a further Board action. This provision expires automatically on December 31, 2029 unless the AII Board acts to extend it.[^24]

## ARTICLE V: MEETINGS

**Section 5.1. Meeting frequency.** Each Working Group shall meet at minimum once per calendar quarter. Additional meetings may be called by the Chair or by petition of one-third of active members with five business days' notice. This one-third threshold governs the right to request a meeting; decisions at any meeting require a quorum and voting majority as specified in Sections 6.1 and 6.3.[^25]

**Section 5.2. Remote participation.** Meetings shall be conducted in a manner accessible to geographically distributed members. Remote participation options shall be provided for all meetings.[^26]

**Section 5.3. Agenda and minutes.** The Chair shall distribute a written agenda at least five business days before each scheduled meeting. Minutes shall be recorded, reviewed by the Chair, and distributed to all Working Group members within ten business days of each meeting.[^27]

## ARTICLE VI: QUORUM AND DECISION-MAKING

**Section 6.1. Quorum.** A quorum for the purpose of taking binding votes is a majority of active members, which may include members attending remotely.[^28]

**Section 6.2. Routine decisions.** Routine decisions—including approval of agendas, meeting minutes, work assignments, and draft deliverables for internal circulation—require a simple majority of members present when a quorum is established.[^29]

**Section 6.3. Supermajority decisions.** The following decisions require a two-thirds supermajority of active members present and voting, provided quorum is established: (a) adoption of a final deliverable for publication; (b) amendment of Working Group Operating Procedures; (c) removal of a member; and (d) dissolution of the Working Group.[^30]

**Section 6.4. Consensus-seeking.** AII Working Groups operate on a consensus-seeking model. Before any formal vote, the Chair shall make reasonable effort to identify and address concerns that prevent consensus. Where consensus is not achievable, dissenting views shall be documented in the official record.[^31]

**Section 6.5. Consensus Defined.** For purposes of this Charter, consensus exists when no active member sustains a formal objection after the Chair has made reasonable effort to address the underlying concern. The absence of a formal objection constitutes consensus; a procedural concern raised but subsequently withdrawn does not prevent a finding of consensus. Consensus is distinct from unanimity; a member may express preference for a different outcome while choosing not to sustain a formal objection. Consensus under this Charter is an internal adoption rule for attributing a position to the Working Group. It is not scientific consensus in the Active Inference field and does not establish the scientific validity of the position adopted.[^32]

**Section 6.6. Formal Objection Process.** Any active member may file a formal objection to a proposed action or deliverable by submitting a written statement to the Chair specifying: (a) the provision or proposed action objected to; (b) the member's substantive concern; and (c) the change that would resolve the objection. The Chair shall distribute the formal objection to all active members and shall attempt to resolve it within twenty business days. If the formal objection is not resolved, it shall be documented in the official record and, where applicable, in the published deliverable.[^33]

**Section 6.7. Minority Report.** When a Working Group adopts a final deliverable over a sustained formal objection, the member or members who sustained the objection may file a minority report. The minority report shall be a brief written statement of the dissenting view. Minority reports shall be included as a separately identified appendix to the published deliverable, clearly labeled as the view of dissenting members and not as a Working Group position.[^34]

**Section 6.8. Abstentions and Recusals.** An abstention is recorded when a member is present and chooses not to vote; abstentions count neither for nor against the motion. A recusal occurs when a member is required to remove themselves from a specific deliberation and vote due to a conflict of interest under Article IX; recusals reduce the quorum requirement proportionally—quorum is calculated based on active members eligible to vote on that specific matter.[^35]

**Section 6.9. Asynchronous Voting.** For routine decisions that do not require deliberation, the Chair may conduct an asynchronous vote by distributing the motion to all active members by email or equivalent documented channel. Asynchronous votes shall remain open for at least five business days. Votes not cast within the open period are treated as non-votes and do not count toward or against either quorum or the required majority. An asynchronous vote is binding only if at least a quorum of active members as defined in Section 6.1 cast votes within the open period. Asynchronous voting is not available for adoption of final deliverables, amendment of Operating Procedures, removal of members, or dissolution of the Working Group, which require deliberative votes at a meeting where quorum is established.[^36]

## ARTICLE VII: DELIVERABLES AND REPORTING

**Section 7.1. Year One Work Plan.** Each Working Group shall adopt a Year One Work Plan at or before its second regular meeting. The Work Plan shall specify the products authorized for that group in Article II.2, the timeline for each, and the member assignments responsible for each. The Work Plan may add further work of the same type if the addition does not displace the named products. Certificate design, tool ownership, and standards drafting require rechartering. A domain roadmap may be assigned only in a subsequent-year Work Plan, and only after the domain working group has completed its initial assessment.[^37]

**Section 7.2. Quarterly reports.** Working Group Chairs shall submit a brief written report to the AII Liaison no less than quarterly. The report shall cover: meetings held and attendance; progress on Year One deliverables; emerging issues or resource needs; and any matters requiring AII Board awareness or action.[^38]

**Section 7.3. Publication review.** Final deliverables shall be submitted to the AII Liaison for review against AII's publication standards and IP Policy prior to publication. AII is the exclusive official publisher of those deliverables. AII reserves the right to request revisions for consistency with institutional policies, accuracy of institutional representations, or adherence to the access and licensing terms in Section 2.3. Responsibility for content, technical analysis, and conclusions remains with the Working Group. AII administrative review shall not substitute institutional judgment for Working Group scientific inquiry.[^39]

**Section 7.4. Output identification.** Published Working Group outputs shall identify the Working Group by name, acknowledge AII as the convening organization, and carry a publication date and version number consistent with AII's document control conventions.[^40]

**Section 7.5. Official outputs.** Official Working Group outputs are the AII-published reports, protocols, frameworks, and analogous deliverables adopted under this Charter. Journal articles and conference papers by individual contributors are not official Working Group outputs. They may follow ICMJE or comparable scholarly authorship standards.[^41] Official outputs shall attribute contributors through a Contributors section, with the Working Group identified as the producing body and AII identified as the publishing organization.[^42]

**Section 7.6. External Review.** The Field Working Group's terminology correspondence matrix and comparison protocol shall be reviewed by at least two reviewers who are not AII Board members and who were not drafters. The assessment framework is adopted by Field Working Group concurrence with constituted domain groups under Article II.2. Domain go/no-go statements shall be published with the membership count, a statement that the expert–practitioner complement required in Article III.4(b) remains present, and any dual-literacy individuals among the members.[^43]

## ARTICLE VIII: INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY

**Section 8.1. Contribution agreement.** Intellectual property contributed to or created within the Working Group process is governed by AII's Intellectual Property Policy (AII-POL-IP-001). Members contributing original content to Working Group deliverables shall execute an IP Contribution Agreement in the form specified by that policy. Where a member's contributions are made in the course of their employment, the member represents that they have obtained any employer authorization required to contribute that content to the Working Group process under these terms, or that no such authorization is required. AII is not responsible for resolving disputes between members and their employers regarding the ownership of contributed content.[^44]

**Section 8.2. Authorship credit.** Working Group members retain authorship credit for their contributions to published deliverables, consistent with the authorship standards the Working Group adopts in its Operating Procedures.[^45]

**Section 8.3. Exclusive official publisher.** AII is the exclusive official publisher of Working Group outputs produced under its institutional imprint. Prior to AII publication, Working Group members and contributors may not publish the same substantive content elsewhere without written consent of the AII Board. "Same substantive content" means the Working Group's actual adopted deliverable text. It leaves untouched derivative works, applications of the Working Group's findings, and scholarly publications that cite or build upon Working Group outputs without reproducing the adopted text. After AII publication under the access and licensing terms in Section 2.3, third parties—including Working Group members and contributors—may use those outputs as that license or access term allows, provided attribution to AII and the relevant Working Group is maintained.[^46]

**Section 8.4. Community resource.** No Working Group member or contributor may claim proprietary rights over content developed within the Working Group process. Content developed through the Working Group process is a community resource: it may not be transferred to the exclusive ownership or control of any individual, institution, or organization outside the access and licensing terms in Section 2.3.[^47]

**Section 8.5. IP policy pending.** AII's full Intellectual Property Policy (AII-POL-IP-001) is in development and will be adopted by the AII Board before the final Charter is ratified. AII-POL-IP-001 will be made publicly available before that ratification vote, and the IP Contribution Agreement referenced in Section 8.1 will be consistent with its terms. If any provision of the finally adopted AII-POL-IP-001 conflicts with this Charter, the IP Policy controls for IP matters and this Charter controls for governance matters. AII will provide written notice to Working Group members of the finally adopted AII-POL-IP-001 at least thirty days before the ratification vote.[^48]

## ARTICLE IX: CONFLICT OF INTEREST

**Section 9.1. Disclosure.** Working Group members shall disclose to the Chair any financial, professional, or personal interest that could affect, or could reasonably appear to affect, their objectivity in a Working Group decision or deliverable. Disclosure shall be made promptly upon the member becoming aware of the potential conflict.[^49]

**Section 9.2. Recusal.** A member with a disclosed conflict shall recuse themselves from the relevant deliberation and vote. The recusal and its basis shall be recorded in the meeting minutes.[^50]

**Section 9.3. Undisclosed conflicts.** Undisclosed conflicts that subsequently come to light shall be reviewed by the AII Liaison and the Chair jointly. If the review finds that a material conflict was not disclosed, the matter may be referred to the AII Board.[^51]

**Section 9.4. Donations not a condition.** Financial contribution to AII, including donation, is not a condition of membership, speaking, or participation.[^52]

## ARTICLE X: AMENDMENT

**Section 10.1. Charter amendment.** This Charter may be amended by a two-thirds supermajority vote of the combined active membership of all constituted Working Groups, provided that the proposed amendment has been circulated to all Working Group members at least thirty days before the vote, and that the AII Board has reviewed and concurred with the proposed change.[^53]

**Section 10.2. Operating Procedures.** Supplementary Operating Procedures for each Working Group may be adopted and amended by a two-thirds supermajority of that Working Group's active members, with notification to the AII Liaison. Operating Procedures may not conflict with this Charter or with AII's governing documents.[^54]

**Section 10.3. Domain Charter Template and Compatibility.** Each domain Working Group operates under a charter derived from the Domain Working Group Charter Template. A domain Working Group may adopt supplementary provisions or modifications, provided they remain compatible with this Charter's open-membership, publication, shared-assessment-framework, and stop-rule provisions. Compatibility is determined by the Field Working Group as a process check. Compatibility review does not extend to scientific or technical judgments of the domain Working Group. Field products are adopted by the Field Working Group, with notice to constituted domain groups. Domain products are adopted by that domain Working Group.[^55]

## ARTICLE XI: DISSOLUTION AND WITHDRAWAL OF THE CONVENER

**Section 11.1. Dissolution.** A Working Group may be dissolved only by a two-thirds supermajority vote of its active members. The AII Board may not dissolve a Working Group.[^56]

**Section 11.2. Records on dissolution.** Upon self-dissolution, if AII is then the convener, the Working Group's official records shall be transferred to AII for archiving, and any unpublished deliverables in progress shall be documented with their current status. If AII is not then the convener, the records remain with the Working Group unless its members transfer them.[^57]

**Section 11.3. Withdrawal of AII as Convener.** AII may withdraw as convening and publishing organization by published Board action, with at least thirty days' written notice to all Working Group members. Withdrawal does not dissolve any Working Group. After withdrawal, a Working Group may continue independently, adopt another convening organization, or dissolve itself under Section 11.1. AII-published outputs already released remain AII publications. Subsequent outputs are not AII publications unless a later convening agreement so provides.[^58]

**Section 11.4. Inactive Groups.** If a Working Group fails to hold any meeting within any consecutive six-month period, or fails to submit required quarterly reports under Section 7.2 for two consecutive reporting periods, the AII Liaison shall provide written notice to the Working Group and a thirty-day period to remedy the deficiency. If the deficiency is not remedied within thirty days of notice, AII may suspend its facilitation and publication for that group. AII may not restructure the group's leadership or membership, and may not dissolve the group. The AII Board shall provide written notice of any such action to all Working Group members.[^59]

## ARTICLE XII: PILOT EVALUATION, STOP RULE, AND SUNSET REVIEW

**Section 12.1. Twelve-Month Pilot Evaluation.** No later than twelve months after the initial constitution of the Working Groups at the November 2026 Symposium, the AII Board of Directors and the Chairs of all constituted Working Groups shall conduct a Pilot Evaluation of the Working Group system. The Pilot Evaluation shall assess: (a) whether each group produced the year-one products named for that group in Article II.2; (b) whether those products were used by at least one intended external audience, or received the required external review; (c) whether working groups have achieved and maintained minimum activity standards under Article III; (d) whether the governance structure in the Charter and Operating Procedures is functioning as intended or requires amendment; and (e) whether the AII coordination infrastructure is adequately supporting working group operations. Completion of the assessment framework and of domain self-assessments is a year-one product test. Stage ratings under the framework are analytical results and are not themselves a continuation test.[^60]

**Section 12.2. Stop Rule.** Continuation of a Working Group, and constitution of any additional domain group, shall not rest solely on a confirmation of the observations in Section 2 of the Position Paper. A group that has not produced its named year-one products shall be paused. For the Field Working Group, the terminology matrix and the comparison protocol must also receive the external review required in Article VII.6. AII shall suspend facilitation and publication for a paused group unless the Working Group and the AII Board concur in a published, time-limited exception stating what product is still due and by when. AII may not dissolve the group. The Field Working Group may be rechartered only if the comparison protocol has been used by at least two implementation communities and has received the external review required in Article VII.6. A domain group may be rechartered only after a published go/no-go recommending continuation. A domain roadmap may be taken up only after that go/no-go and only as a subsequent-year Work Plan decision. Rechartering is a new decision. A change in the named object, or the addition of a new class of work other than a post-assessment domain roadmap, requires rechartering under Article II.[^61]

**Section 12.3. Five-Year Sunset Review.** No later than five years after the initial constitution of the Working Groups at the November 2026 Symposium, and every five years thereafter, the AII Board of Directors and the collective active membership of all then-constituted Working Groups shall conduct a comprehensive Sunset Review of the Working Group system.[^62]

**Section 12.4. Sunset Review criteria.** The five-year Sunset Review shall assess: (a) whether the Working Group system continues to serve its stated purposes and the active inference community's needs; (b) whether the Field charter and domain-charter template should be retained, narrowed, or expanded; (c) whether the governance structure, including this Charter, requires substantive revision; and (d) whether any Working Groups that remain constituted continue to fulfill their mandates productively.[^63]

**Section 12.5. Public sunset report.** The five-year Sunset Review shall produce a public report available to the active inference community within thirty days of completion. Recommended changes to this Charter shall be proposed under the amendment process in Article X. The first five-year Sunset Review is due no later than November 2031.[^64]
