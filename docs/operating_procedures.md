These Operating Procedures supplement the Working Group Charter ([Appendix A](working_group_charter.md)). Where any provision of these Procedures conflicts with the Charter, the Charter controls. Individual Working Groups may adopt Working-Group-specific supplementary procedures, provided those procedures are consistent with both this document and the Charter, and are filed with the AII Liaison.[^1]

## 1. Meeting Conduct

1.1. The Chair opens each meeting by confirming quorum and reviewing the agenda. Agenda modifications require the consent of a simple majority of members present.[^2]

1.2. Members are expected to arrive prepared, having reviewed any pre-circulated materials. Discussion time at meetings is for deliberation, not for first reading of documents.[^3]

1.3. The Chair facilitates discussion to ensure equitable participation. Any member may call a point of order if they believe the meeting is not being conducted in accordance with the Charter or these Procedures.[^4]

1.4. Meetings shall not exceed two hours without explicit consent of a majority of members present. Extended working sessions may be scheduled separately.[^5]

## 2. Agenda and Minutes

2.1. The Chair circulates a draft agenda no later than five business days before each meeting, inviting additions from members.[^6]

2.2. The Chair designates a note-taker for each meeting. Minutes shall record: (a) date, time, and participants; (b) agenda items discussed; (c) decisions taken and vote counts for formal votes; (d) action items with assigned owners and due dates; and (e) any conflicts of interest disclosed and recusals taken. Minutes shall not be verbatim transcripts of deliberations.[^7]

2.3. Draft minutes are circulated to all Working Group members within ten business days of each meeting. Members have ten additional business days to submit written corrections. Corrected minutes are approved at the following meeting.[^8]

## 3. Deliverable Development Process

3.1. Each deliverable shall have a designated Lead Member responsible for coordinating its development, maintaining the working draft, and managing the review cycle. Deliverables shall match the products authorized for that Working Group in Charter Article II.2.[^9]

3.2. Working drafts are internal Working Group documents and shall be clearly marked as such. No working draft may be represented externally as a Working Group position or AII publication.[^10]

3.3. Before a deliverable proceeds to a final adoption vote, it shall undergo at least one structured internal review cycle in which all active members have the opportunity to submit written comments. The Lead Member shall prepare a written response to all substantive comments received.[^11]

3.4. The Chair may invite SAB members or external domain experts to review a deliverable before final adoption. External reviewers shall be disclosed in the published document's acknowledgments.[^12]

3.5. After the Working Group adopts a final deliverable, the Chair submits it to the AII Liaison for institutional review per Article VII.3 of the Charter. The AII Liaison shall complete institutional review within twenty business days.[^13]

## 4. Authorship and Attribution

4.1. Authorship of Working Group deliverables is determined by the Working Group using criteria consistent with recognized academic standards for scholarly authorship (ICMJE criteria: substantive intellectual contribution to conception or design, or to data acquisition, analysis, or interpretation; AND drafting or critical revision of the work; AND approval of the final version; AND accountability for the work). Members who contributed only through meeting participation, administrative support, or general direction shall be acknowledged but are not listed as authors.[^14]

4.2. Published deliverables shall identify the Working Group by name, list the authors in the order agreed by the Working Group, and acknowledge AII as the institutional publisher. The publication date, version number, and DOI (once assigned) shall appear on the document.[^15]

## 5. Communication and Transparency

5.1. Working Group business is conducted through channels designated by the Chair and accessible to all active members. Minutes, Work Plans, and adopted deliverables shall be filed with the AII Liaison and made available on AII's public platforms upon adoption.[^16]

5.2. Working Group members may communicate publicly about their personal expertise and about published Working Group outputs. Members shall not characterize Working Group positions or AII positions on matters that have not been formally adopted.[^17]

5.3. Media inquiries about Working Group activities shall be referred to the AII Liaison. Individual members may speak to the media in their personal capacity, provided they make clear that they do not speak for the Working Group or for AII.[^18]

## 6. Onboarding New Members

6.1. New members shall be provided with: (a) the Working Group Charter and these Operating Procedures; (b) the Working Group's current Work Plan and the minutes of the three most recent meetings; (c) a brief orientation from the Chair or Vice Chair covering the Working Group's current stage of work.[^19]

6.2. New members are encouraged to observe one meeting before taking on active assignments, though this is not required.[^20]

## 7. Member Participation and Inactivity

7.1. Active membership as defined in Article III.3 of the Charter requires attendance at one meeting and contribution to one deliverable activity per six-month period. The Chair shall track participation and notify members approaching inactive status with at least thirty days' advance notice.[^21]

7.2. A member who has become inactive may be reinstated to active status by attending one meeting and notifying the Chair of their intention to resume active participation.[^22]

7.3. If an officer becomes inactive for sixty or more consecutive days without prior notice, the Vice Chair may assume the Chair's duties on an interim basis and the Working Group shall schedule an election at its next meeting.[^23]

## 8. Review Cadence for These Procedures

8.1. These Operating Procedures shall be reviewed by the collective Working Groups no later than twelve months after initial adoption and annually thereafter. Proposed amendments shall be circulated to all Working Group members at least thirty days before any vote.[^24]

[^2]: Implements Charter §§6.1–6.2. TAB §2.5 distinguishes action items from information items and requires approval of minutes at the next meeting. Opening by confirming quorum is the local chairing rule; it is not copied from a predecessor sentence.

[^3]: RFC 2418 §3.1: the agenda should indicate what documents participants must read to be prepared. RFC 2418 §3.2: the Chair may refuse the floor to a person who is unprepared. The “no first reading” sentence applies those rules to meeting time.

[^4]: RFC 2418 §6.1: the Chair is responsible for a fair and open process. W3C §5.2.1: Chairs must ensure that legitimate views and objections are considered. A point of order is ordinary parliamentary practice; it is not taken from IEEE TAB, RFC 2418, or W3C Process.

[^5]: No predecessor caps meeting length. The two-hour default exists because the membership is geographically distributed and remote. A majority of those present may extend; longer work is scheduled separately so ordinary meetings stay usable.

[^6]: Same lineage as Charter §5.3: RFC 2418 §7.1 (two weeks), W3C §3.1.2.1 (two weeks face-to-face; 24 hours distributed), TAB §2.5.C (ten calendar days). Five business days and an invitation for member additions are the local choice.

[^7]: RFC 2418 §3.1: minutes should include the agenda, an account of discussion and decisions, and attendees. TAB §2.5.D: minutes record attendance, the substance of discussions, and actions, and need not be a verbatim transcript. IRS sample policy Art. IV requires recording of conflicts and recusals. Items (a)–(e) combine those rules. The last sentence follows TAB’s non-verbatim rule.

[^8]: TAB §2.5.D: minutes are distributed before the next regular meeting and approved there. W3C §3.1.2.1: minutes two weeks after a face-to-face meeting. Ten business days to circulate and ten to correct, with approval at the following meeting, are the local windows.

[^9]: RFC 2418 §6.3: a group designates an Editor; as a general practice the Chair and Document Editor are different people. W3C §6.2.1: each Technical Report is edited by one or more editors appointed by the Chair. “Lead Member” is that role. Matching Charter Article II.2 products implements the charter-as-contract rule.

[^10]: W3C §6.3.1: Editor’s drafts have no official standing and do not imply Working Group or W3C endorsement; Working Drafts do not necessarily represent consensus. The marking and non-representation rule applies that distinction to AII working drafts.

[^11]: W3C §6.2.2: Working Groups should formally address substantive review comments in a timely manner. ANSI ER §§1.6, 2.6: prompt consideration of written views and objections, with written disposition and reasons. One structured internal cycle and a written response to every substantive comment are the local application.

[^12]: RFC 2418 §8 Last-Call and W3C §6.2.2.1 wide review are community-scale checks. This section makes SAB or external expert review optional with the Chair, and requires disclosure in acknowledgments. It does not replace Charter §7.6’s mandatory Field external review.

[^13]: Implements Charter §7.3. W3C §6.2.1: the Team may refuse a nonconforming publication. The twenty-business-day Liaison clock is proposal-specific. IEEE-SA §5.1.2.1(p)(5)’s twenty-day access clock is a different obligation and is not the source.

[^14]: ICMJE four criteria, quoted in the section: substantial contribution to conception, design, or data; drafting or critical revision; final approval; and accountability… (continued on next page) ([https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/browse/roles-and-responsibilities/defining-the-role-of-authors-and-contributors.html](https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/browse/roles-and-responsibilities/defining-the-role-of-authors-and-contributors.html)). ICMJE: persons who provided only general supervision, administrative support, or meeting participation are acknowledged, not listed as authors. Already cited in the Charter as note 75.

[^15]: Implements Charter §7.4. W3C §6.2.1 publication rules require public, identifiable Technical Reports. Author order by Working Group agreement, AII as institutional publisher, and date, version, and DOI are the local identification block.

[^16]: RFC 2418 treats the mailing list as the standing venue and requires minutes to be posted. W3C §7.1: public communication; technical reports are public. TAB §2.5 requires distribution of minutes. Designated channels accessible to all active members, and public posting of minutes, Work Plans, and adopted deliverables, apply those rules to AII platforms.

[^17]: No predecessor states this speech rule in these terms. It exists so members can discuss their own expertise and published outputs without characterizing a Working Group or AII position that has not been adopted. It is the public-facing counterpart of Operating Procedures §3.2.

[^18]: W3C §3.4.1: the Team Contact is the interface to the rest of the organization. Referral of media inquiries to the AII Liaison is that function. Personal-capacity speech with a clear disclaimer is proposal-specific.

[^19]: No predecessor specifies an onboarding packet. The clause exists because open membership (Charter §3.1; ANSI ER §1.1) only works if a new member can recover the Charter, these Procedures, the current Work Plan, recent minutes, and the group’s present stage.

[^20]: No predecessor requires a waiting meeting. Observation is encouraged, not required, so onboarding does not become a waiting-period barrier contrary to ANSI ER §1.1 openness.

[^21]: Implements Charter §3.3. Thirty days’ notice before inactive status is proposal-specific due process. RFC 2418 has no roster and therefore no inactivity track.

[^22]: No predecessor states a reinstatement rule. Inactivity is a status, not expulsion under Charter §3.7. Attendance at one meeting plus notice to the Chair restores active status so the six-month test can restart without a new admission vote.

[^23]: RFC 2418 §4 allows the Area Director to choose new Chairs if the group cannot complete its work. This Charter keeps officer selection with the group (Charter §4.1). Sixty days’ officer inactivity, interim Vice Chair service, and election at the next meeting are the local continuity rule.

[^24]: W3C §11: Process revision requires Advisory Committee review of at least 28 days. Charter §10.2: Operating Procedures are amended by two-thirds, with Liaison notification. A twelve-month first review, annual reviews thereafter, and thirty days’ circulation are the local cadence. W3C Process does not require annual review of group procedures.
